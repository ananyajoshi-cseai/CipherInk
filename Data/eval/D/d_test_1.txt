THE INIMITABLE JEEVES * * * * * * WHAT THIS BOOK IS ABOUT When either Bertie Wooster or his friends found themselves in the soup or in dangerous proximity to the tureen, the instinct of one and all was to turn to Jeeves--Bertie’s man. He understood human nature, especially that of gilded youth. It did not matter if the hope of an ancient house had fallen in love with a waitress, or if Bertie’s cousins Claude and Eustace had been playing dido; Jeeves never failed. His was a sound brain. The only thing in which Jeeves failed, that is in his master’s eyes, was that he could not always go the whole way with him in the matter of spats, socks and ties, particularly in the Spring--Jeeves was a purist.
